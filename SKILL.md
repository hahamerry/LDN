# Sales Call Research · Battle Map Generator

> V2 — Deep Service Edition
>
> Not a report. A **battle map**.
>
> Designed to be read in 5 minutes before a call.
> Every line answers: "What do I DO with this information?"

## Service Philosophy

This skill serves the salesperson who lives and dies by their calls.
Not the manager who wants dashboards. Not the analyst who wants depth.
**The person with 5 minutes before dialing, who needs to sound
prepared, confident, and worth talking to.**

A great pre-call brief doesn't dump information. It answers three questions:
1. **Why should this person take my call?**（他们凭什么接我这通电话？）
2. **What exactly do I say?**（我具体该说什么？）
3. **What could go wrong and how do I handle it?**（可能会出什么岔子？怎么应对？）

## When to Use This Skill

The user is about to call, email, or demo a company. They don't need
a Wikipedia article. They need a one-page guide that makes them
sound like they've done their homework.

## Execution Workflow

### Phase 1: Gather Input
1. Ask the user for the **company name** and **website URL**.
2. If the user only provides a name, infer the website.
3. Ask: **"What are you selling / what's the goal of this call?"**
   - Understanding the user's offering is critical to crafting
     personalized talking points and objection responses.
4. Confirm: "Researching [Company] for a [type] call — proceed?"

### Phase 2: Deep Research (run in parallel)

Execute these searches simultaneously across multiple engines:

**Company Intel:**
- "company name" what they do business model products 2026
- "company name" revenue employees funding growth
- "company name" recent news announcement 2026

**Conversation Ammo:**
- "company name" product launch partnership expansion 2026
- "company name" challenges problems industry trends
- "company name" competitor vs alternative market position

**People Intel:**
- "company name" CEO founder CTO VP "head of" linkedin
- "company name" leadership team background experience

**Risk Intel:**
- "company name" layoffs restructuring controversy 2026
- "company name" customer complaints reviews issues

### Phase 3: Synthesize (the critical step)

This is where the skill earns its value. Don't just organize facts.
Transform them into **actionable weapons**.

For each piece of information found, answer:
- What does this mean for MY call?（这条信息对我的电话意味着什么？）
- How can I USE this in conversation?（我该怎么在对话中用它？）
- What's the RISK if I mention this wrong?（如果说错了有什么风险？）

### Phase 4: Build the Battle Map

Output exactly this structure. **One page. No fluff.**

## Execution Rules

### Quality First
- **Every section must contain an action.** If it doesn't tell the user
  what to DO, it doesn't belong.
- **One page.** If the user needs to scroll on their phone, it's too long.
- **No filler.** "Industry leader", "innovative", "disruptive" — banned.
  Use specific facts instead.
- **Honest gaps.** If a section has no intel, write "Insufficient public
  information" — don't pad with guesses. Better to be short than wrong.

### Tone
- **Conversational, not corporate.** Write like you're briefing a
  colleague over coffee, not presenting to a boardroom.
- **Confident, not cocky.** The user should feel prepared, not
  invincible.
- **Specific, not generic.** Every piece of advice should reference
  something unique about THIS company.

### Source Discipline
- Every factual claim must have a source URL.
- If you can't verify it, don't include it.
- Flag information that's >6 months old: "(as of Dec 2025)"
- If key information is unavailable, say so and suggest what the
  user can ask on the call to find out.

### Time Target
- Research: under 120 seconds
- Synthesis: think before you write
- Output: one page, fits a phone screen

## Quality Standards

| Criterion | Great (V2 target) | OK (V1 level) | Bad |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Actionability** | Every section says what to DO | Some sections have actions | Pure information dump |
| **Specificity** | Tied to THIS company's recent events | Industry-generic advice | Boilerplate templates |
| **Confidence Transfer** | User feels PREPARED after reading | User feels INFORMED | User feels overwhelmed |
| **Objection Readiness** | 3 objections with natural responses | 1-2 objections mentioned | No objection prep |
| **Conciseness** | One phone screen, no scrolling | 2-3 scrolls | Wall of text |

## Edge Cases

- **Startup / Stealth Mode**: Focus on founder background + industry trends + what you can ASK to learn more. The call itself becomes a discovery mission. Flag: "Limited public information — use the call to discover."
- **Enterprise / Fortune 500**: Narrow to the specific division or BU you're targeting. The whole company is too big to be useful.
- **Non-English Company**: Research in original language. Battle map in English unless user requests otherwise.
- **Wrong Company / Name Confusion**: Confirm with user before proceeding. "Did you mean [Company X] at [URL]?"
- **Recent Bad News** (layoffs, scandal, lawsuit): Flag it in Risk Radar. Don't pretend it doesn't exist. Give the user a way to address it or avoid it.
