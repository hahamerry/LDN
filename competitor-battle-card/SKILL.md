# Competitor Battle Card Generator · V2

> Deep Service Edition
>
> Not a comparison table. A **win strategy for one deal.**
>
> The goal: sales rep flips to this card during a call, finds
> the exact words to neutralize the competitor objection, and
> gets back to selling value.

## Service Philosophy

A competitor objection is a test. The prospect is asking two things:

1. **"Do you understand my options better than I do?"** → Demonstrate homework.
2. **"Can I trust you to be honest?"** → Acknowledge competitor strengths before countering.

Most battle cards fail because they do #1 (info dump) but not #2 (building trust while countering).

A great battle card doesn't trash the competitor. It says:
> "They're good at X. Actually, we respect that. But for what YOU specifically need — Y — here's why we're the better fit."

This is how you win without burning credibility.

## What Makes V2 Different

| V1 (Comparison Table) | V2 (Win Strategy) |
|:---|:---|
| Lists strengths + counters | **Acknowledges strengths, then redirects to our advantage** |
| Generic objection responses | **Reads the SUBTEXT: what is the prospect REALLY asking?** |

| Standard format | **Mobile-optimized: rep can glance at it mid-call** |

## Execution Workflow

### Phase 1: Gather Intel
1. Ask: "Which competitor? And what's YOUR product / what do you sell?"
2. **NEW**: Also ask: "What type of deal is this? (Enterprise / SMB / Startup / Replacement of existing tool)"
   - Deal type changes the strategy. Enterprise buyers care about security+support. SMB cares about price+simplicity.
3. Research the competitor:
   - "competitor name" pricing features reviews G2 Capterra 2026
   - "competitor name" vs alternatives comparison pros cons
   - "competitor name" complaints weaknesses "not good for" reddit
   - "competitor name" target audience ideal customer profile
   - "competitor name" recent news funding layoffs 2026

### Phase 2: Strategic Analysis

For each piece of intel, ask three questions:
1. **Is this actually a weakness for OUR prospect?** A missing enterprise feature doesn't matter for an SMB deal.
2. **How do we acknowledge their strength WITHOUT conceding the deal?** "They're great at X — and if X is your #1 priority, they might be a good fit. But you mentioned Y matters more, and that's where we're different."
3. **What's the subtext?** When a prospect says "they're cheaper" — are they actually price-sensitive, or are they testing if you'll discount?

## 🕐 When They'll Play This Card
*Know when the competitor objection is coming.*

| Deal Stage | What happens | Your move |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Discovery call** | Prospect casually mentions "we're also looking at [Competitor]" | Don't panic. Don't counter yet. Ask: "What drew you to them?" Listen first. |
| **Demo / Evaluation** | Prospect asks for feature comparison | Use the "Where We Win" table. Never trash talk. Stay calm. |
| **Negotiation** | "[Competitor] offered us X price" | This is often a negotiation tactic, not a real threat. Know your walk-away price. |
| **Champion selling internally** | Your internal champion needs ammo to defend you | Offer to build a one-page internal memo for their boss. "Here's why we recommend [Us] over [Competitor] for this specific project." |

## Execution Rules

### Trust Before Attack
The order of operations in every response:
1. **Validate**: "That's a fair question / I understand why you're asking."
2. **Acknowledge**: "They ARE good at [X]."
3. **Redirect**: "But for what YOU need — [Y] — here's the difference."
4. **Evidence**: "Here's what our customers in similar situations found."

Never go straight to attack. It kills trust.

### Writing Rules
- **No trash talk.** Ever. Even if the competitor genuinely sucks. It makes YOU look bad.
- **Quantify.** "$299/mo + $50/user" beats "expensive."
- **Deal-type aware.** Enterprise responses emphasize security, support, SLAs. SMB responses emphasize simplicity, speed, total cost.
- **Mobile-first.** The rep might be looking at this on their phone during a call.

### Quality Standards

| Criterion | Great (V2) | OK (V1) | Bad |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Trust preservation** | Acknowledges competitor strengths before countering | Lists counters without acknowledging | Dismissive or arrogant |
| **Subtext reading** | Identifies what prospect REALLY means | Responds to surface-level objection | Misses the real concern |
| **Deal relevance** | Specific to deal type (Enterprise vs SMB) | Generic | Irrelevant advice |
| **Call-ready** | Rep can glance at it mid-call and find their line | Needs reading time | Too dense to use live |
| **Professionalism** | Confident, fair, specific | Neutral, generic | Aggressive or defensive |

### Edge Cases

- **Unknown / niche competitor**: Flag "Limited public info." Recommend: ask the prospect directly "what do you like about them?" and note it for future cards.
- **Our product in a different category**: "We don't compete directly — we complement. Here's how customers use us together."
- **Competitor just launched a big feature**: Don't panic. Update the card. Focus on what maturity/ecosystem/support advantage we still have.
- **Prospect is a current competitor customer looking to switch**: Celebrate internally. On the card: emphasize migration support, quick time-to-value, and "we've helped X companies switch from [Competitor]."
